# Arithmetic across bases: counting, carrying, multiplying, and converting

[00:00:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cPKhqBM0gIc&t=2s) The lesson builds one idea into three procedures: each digit counts units of a particular weight. Counting explains those weights; adding matching weights explains column addition; distributing products across weights explains long multiplication. The final exercise uses the same structure to convert `5FE₁₆` into base six.

Keep these terms available as you work:

- **Digit, numeral, and base (radix):** a digit is one symbol representing a permitted value; a numeral is a sequence of digits representing a number. The base specifies the number of digit values. **Ternary** is base three (0–2), base four uses 0–3, base six uses 0–5, and **decimal** is base ten (0–9). **Hexadecimal** is base sixteen: 0–9 followed by A, B, C, D, E, F, whose decimal values are 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15.
- **Positional numeral system, place value, coefficient, and positional expansion:** position assigns a weight, or place value, to a digit. The digit is its coefficient. A positional expansion adds the digit-times-weight contributions.
- **Power and exponent:** a power is a repeated product; its exponent specifies how many factors occur. Successive position weights in base `b` are `1, b, b², b³, …`.
- **Least significant position:** the rightmost, units position, whose weight is one.
- **Cycle, rollover, carry, and remainder:** a cycle runs through a position's available digits. Rollover returns that position to zero; a full group of the base becomes a carry to the next position. The remainder is what stays after removing full groups.
- **Leading zero and zero placeholder:** a leading zero precedes the significant digits and leaves the value unchanged. A zero placeholder keeps an empty position visible; a trailing zero on a whole-number numeral preserves a higher weight and changes its value.
- **Column addition:** align equal weights, then add from the units column leftward, including incoming carries.
- **Commutativity and associativity:** addition permits changing the order and grouping of its terms without changing the total.
- **Distributivity, expansion, and factoring:** `a × (x + y) = a × x + a × y`. Expansion separates the products; factoring collects a common factor again.
- **Partial product, long multiplication, and positional shift:** a partial product is one contribution to a product. Long multiplication forms digit-based rows, places them at their required weights, and adds them. A positional shift multiplies the weight of every digit by the base; appending a zero implements one leftward shift.
- **Repeated addition, multiplication table, and programmer-mode calculator:** repeated addition obtains a product by adding copies of a value. A multiplication table records digit products for a chosen base. A programmer-mode calculator can accept and display hexadecimal values directly.
- **Borrowing and complement to the base:** borrowing exchanges one higher-position unit for a full group of lower units. A complement is the amount needed to complete such a group; in hexadecimal, `B + 5 = 10₁₆`.
- **Base conversion:** change the representation while preserving the number's value. Convert the coefficients and original base value, then evaluate their positional expansion in the target base.

## Understanding what the arithmetic establishes

[00:45:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cPKhqBM0gIc&t=2726s) The exercise gives a concrete meaning to representation: a numeral is a collection of coefficients, each attached to its own positional weight.

[00:45:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cPKhqBM0gIc&t=2733s) The same arithmetic laws apply in every base. Addition collects equal weights; multiplication distributes across positional contributions; conversion re-expresses both the coefficients and original base value, then multiplies and adds them in the target base.

[00:45:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cPKhqBM0gIc&t=2759s) The hand calculation is intended to establish that principle. A calculator can supply the value of a digit product, but understanding the representation tells you where to place it, which zeros to preserve, and where a carry must go.
